Where the numbers come from. Mass, reference area and the Cx0(Mach) drag curve are read from the weapon table, along with the loft angle, loft-off range, nav gain, g limit and fuze radius. The Cx0 Mach step is taken from fm.table_scale when present.

Motor thrust comes from specific impulse. The table's impulse field is Isp in seconds, not total impulse in newton-seconds — thrust = Isp × g₀ × (fuel mass ÷ burn time). For the AIM-120C that's ~18.1 kN, close to an independent cross-check of solving for the thrust needed to reach the table's own Mach_max by burnout (~23.6 kN, 23% apart). If a loaded weapon has no usable Isp/fuel data, it falls back to that Mach_max calibration and says so.

Every aircraft flies a sequence of phases, not a reaction. Intercept is the mandatory baseline from launch; Crank (no trigger of its own) and Out (its own trigger) are independent toggles layered on top — check both and the aircraft cranks from launch, then breaks into Out once its trigger fires, from whatever heading/altitude/speed the crank left it at; Cold takes over the instant Out's heading-hold first settles onto "pointing away." Every phase converges its own speed (via acceleration) and altitude (via climb angle) toward its own targets and holds once reached — re-based from wherever the aircraft actually is the moment that phase takes over, so switching phases is never a discontinuity. All of it is pre-planned from the sliders, computed once up front: no aircraft reacts to the missile(s) in flight beyond its configured phases (no defensive break at seeker activation that isn't already an Out/Cold you set up, no notching).

Intercept, Crank, and Cold all hold a commanded angle off the other aircraft's bearing using the same 3g-capped, bang-bang controller (Out uses its own turn-rate input instead of 3g) — Intercept and Crank reference the angle from pointing at the other aircraft (0° = pure pursuit / hot), Cold references it from pointing away (0° = cold, ±90° = beam, 180° = hot). Start has no heading input at all: each aircraft's initial heading is derived to already match whichever phase governs at launch (Intercept, or Crank if enabled), so a default (0°) head-on setup starts with zero heading error, same as flying straight always meant.

Out always points directly away from the other aircraft, continuously — not a fixed 180° arc computed once at the trigger instant. It's the same heading-hold mechanism as the others, just commanded at 180° and re-aimed every step at wherever the other aircraft actually is, so it keeps tracking "away" even if that aircraft is itself maneuvering. The lateral drift this produces is real motion now, visible in the top-down view (rendered to scale, unlike the side view) rather than a dropped axis.

Out-turn's default trigger is "my own missile went active" — this aircraft's own missile no longer needs radar support to guide, so it breaks off. The hostile's version needs Return fire enabled (otherwise there's no missile of its own to activate, so it never triggers), and the friendly's version needs Friendly fires enabled for the same reason. A plain friendly–hostile range trigger is still available as an alternative. Resolving an activation trigger takes an extra simulation pass or two (a missile's guidance only ever depends on the aircraft it's chasing, never the firer's own maneuvering, so this resolves in a fixed sequence rather than needing to iterate) — one edge case isn't chased further: the friendly's own activation time is resolved against the hostile's pre-break path, so if the hostile's return-fire-triggered break somehow started before the friendly's missile went active, that wouldn't be reflected. Realistically the hostile's own missile can't even activate that early, since it hasn't launched yet at that point.

Which field is "seeker activation" is not documented. Two candidates are marked separately: D_max (16 km here), which behaves like the active-homing handoff, and sensor.sens_far_dist (30 km), the outer detection limit. Both are gated by sensor.delay. Treat the labels as the fields they came from, not as confirmed game behaviour.

Known limits. Induced drag is not modelled, so the missile pays nothing for lift during the loft hold and terminal turn and high-altitude ranges come out optimistic. Rmax is usually limited by Life_Time rather than by energy. Guidance is full 3D proportional navigation, decoupled into independent elevation and azimuth channels sharing one g-limit budget — useful for envelope planning, not a replica of ED's 6-DOF model. The side (range/altitude) view is a projection that ignores cross-range, so it understates true separation once either aircraft cranks or out-turns; the top-down view carries no altitude information. If return fire is enabled, the hostile's shot is guided the same way, independently, against the friendly's own flight path. If Friendly fires is turned off, the friendly aircraft still flies its configured phases -- it just never launches anything.
